Doctor Theodore Penfold

Source: wiki.md

← Back to Characters View relationship charts

Appears in: Couch Potato Crisis

Profile

  • Appears in: Couch Potato Crisis

  • Aliases: Doctor Penfold, Penfold

  • Race: Human

  • Class: Scanmaster

  • Age: 167 years old at time of Crisis

  • Role: Queen Murderjoy’s head scientist, the world’s foremost authority on stat-point mechanics, and architect of the Grey Room personality-conditioning programme

  • Personality: Penfold is defined by a single consuming terror: death. At 167 years old – far beyond any normal human lifespan – he sustains himself through a strict regimen of health potions every twenty minutes, a bed positioned directly over a save point so his health replenishes as he sleeps, and a retinue of healers surrounding him at all times. This extended life has not brought peace; it has sharpened his dread. He drinks whisky for the taste and the burn, knowing the save point negates intoxication – a small comfort for a man who cannot afford to lose a single lucid moment to pleasure. His obsession with immortality has produced a clinical detachment indistinguishable from cruelty. He speaks of killing and resurrecting [Princess Kiwi](./character-princess-kiwistafel-questgiver.html) dozens or hundreds of times as “rolling the dice” on personality stats, and orders Karinla to “kill the subject” with the same tone one might use to restart a malfunctioning machine. He addresses Queen Murderjoy with startling rudeness – “Come again?” and “Bah” – that would earn any other Zhakaran instant incineration, but which she tolerates because his genius is irreplaceable. His paranoia is as thorough as his science: he refuses to be touched while sleeping (servants must ring a bell to wake him), keeps a walking stick close at hand, and has built his entire underground life around proximity to save points. When a brain-transfer experiment leaves the human subject screaming and begging for death before expiring moments later, Penfold calls it “an unqualified success” – because the subject spoke, proving the mind transfer worked. This ability to reframe horrific failure as scientific progress is his defining intellectual trait and his deepest moral failing.

  • Backstory: Penfold is the acknowledged world expert in scanmastery, a class that allows its practitioner to see through a target’s eyes, access their menu interface and inventory, and – at sufficient level – read personality trait values invisible to ordinary observers. He authored Sense and Sense Abilities, A Definitive Guide, the definitive textbook on stat-point theory, which explains the three classifications of stats (authoritative, reflective, and derived) and catalogues over a thousand common stat points. He also developed a custom illusion spell that creates a holographic echo of a scanned target’s menu, allowing him to share scan data with clients visually rather than reading it aloud – an innovation Murderjoy has “never heard of anyone else doing.” How he came to serve Murderjoy is implied rather than stated: she provides unlimited resources for his immortality research – human subjects, assistants, GP, laboratory space, and political protection. In return, he develops weapons of mass destruction for Zhakara’s war effort, including an animated cloud of invisible nerve gas that causes slow, painful death to anyone with elven blood, and “unnatural amalgamations, creatures that be the fused product of men and beasts.” His laboratory is hidden deep in the abandoned [Mines of Bahg Taldur](./location-mines-of-bahg-taldur.html), a former dwarven mithryl and orihalcum mine – chosen because “even Zhakaran cruelty had its limits” and his experiments were too gruesome for any surface facility. He also possesses the “source code for Zhakaran Law,” the spell that powers the legal system governing the slave state, which he later trades to Tasha in exchange for his freedom during the lab raid.

  • Significant story events:

    • Chapter 7 – The Chemist and the Mathemagician (Crisis): Penfold’s textbook Sense and Sense Abilities is excerpted as an interlude, establishing him as the authoritative voice on stat-point mechanics. The excerpt introduces the concepts of authoritative, reflective, and derived stats, and mentions “shadow stats” – personality traits hidden from ordinary folk. This is the reader’s first encounter with Penfold, months before he appears as a character.
    • Chapter 13 – Bad Science, Part 1 (Crisis): Penfold is introduced in person watching Kiwi die in the Grey Room through a one-way mirror. Murderjoy asks whether he can “convince the princess to serve me.” He explains the conditioning process: each death reshuffles personality stats; by killing Kiwi dozens of times and recording the values, he and Karinla can determine the statistical range and wait for a respawn where Love is low, Egocentricity is above 70%, and Loyalty is manageable. He explains that this technique works on elves (whose psychology is “based on logic and reason”) but not humans (whose psychology is “based on habits and learned behaviors”). Karinla is introduced as his uncollared, level-50 assistant. Murderjoy offers him unlimited resources for his immortality research if he succeeds.
    • Chapter 18 – Bad Science, Part 2 (Crisis): Penfold is woken by Deirdre’s bell and walks ten minutes from his save-point apartment to the lab. He examines Kiwi’s latest personality stats with Murderjoy and Karinla; the numbers are close but “her loyalty stat is higher than I’d like.” He orders Kiwi killed again. He then shows Murderjoy his immortality experiments: Karinla surgically transfers a human volunteer’s brain into an elven body while mages bombard the body with healing spells to fuse the brain to the elven spinal cord. The subject wakes screaming, begs for death, and dies moments later. Penfold declares it a success because the mind transfer was confirmed. He plans to eventually perform the surgery using teleportation magic instead of physical incision.
    • Chapter 25 – Beachhead (Crisis): Referenced when Fin confirms that Gelkorus has taken Kiwi to “[Doctor Penfold’s lab](./location-penfold-s-underground-lab.html).” Tasha asks for directions and begins planning the raid.
    • Chapter 27 – Knight of the Metal Joints (Crisis): Tasha’s party travels toward [the [Mines of Bahg Taldur](./location-mines-of-bahg-taldur.html)](./location-penfold-s-underground-lab.html). Captain K’her describes Penfold as “a mad scientist in the employ of Queen Murderjoy” who “creates weapons of mass destruction” and “unnatural amalgamations.” Hermes identifies the mine from a dwarven tome. The stage is set for the lab raid.
    • Chapter 28 (Crisis): During the raid on the lab, Penfold trades Tasha the “source code for Zhakaran Law” in exchange for his freedom. He and Karinla escape together while Karinla takes the Orb of Life unnoticed from the battlefield.
    • Epilogue (Crisis): The final scene of the book. Penfold lies dying on a wooden table in an abandoned medical tent near the battlefield, coughing blood, his heart failing. He has only a few health potions left. He orders Karinla to perform the brain-transfer operation – this time into Princess Kiwistafel’s dead body, which Karinla has been keeping viable with periodic healing spells. He accepts a neurotoxin injection to the heart. Karinla removes his brain, places it in Kiwi’s skull, and fuses it with enchanted surgical adhesive and her most powerful healing spell, channelled through the stolen Orb of Life. The operation succeeds: Penfold’s mind awakens in Kiwi’s body, in agonising pain that fades as the Orb of Life resonates with “Kiwi-Penfold’s” new form. He sits up, declares “I’m truly immortal,” and achieves his life’s goal – leaving a horrific unresolved threat for Book 3.

  • Notable Quotes:

    “Come again?” – Chapter 13, Bad Science (Crisis). Penfold’s first spoken line, delivered with rude dismissiveness to Queen Murderjoy after she asks him a direct question – establishing that he is the one person in Zhakara who can afford to treat the queen casually.

    “It’s hard to say. Each death is a roll of the dice. I’ll have a better idea of her personality range once we’ve killed her a few dozen times and recorded the stat values.” – Chapter 13, Bad Science (Crisis). Penfold explains the Grey Room conditioning process to Murderjoy, reducing Kiwi’s repeated deaths to a statistical exercise.

    “A failure? Not at all. This was an unqualified success.” – Chapter 18, Bad Science (Crisis). Penfold’s response after a human test subject wakes in an elven body screaming, begs for death, and dies moments later – because the subject spoke, proving the mind transfer worked.

    “Because, your majesty, you’ll only get one chance to convince her. Her low love stat combined with her high egocentricity stat might make her more amicable to your philosophy, but her high loyalty could compel her to refuse to join you because she would see it as a betrayal of her friends. I wouldn’t want to try it unless her loyalty was no higher than 60%.” – Chapter 18, Bad Science (Crisis). Penfold advises patience, explaining the precise statistical threshold for the conditioning to succeed.

    “I’m sure. We have only a few health potions left, and my heart is getting worse. Entropy wants my life, but we shall deny him his prize. Begin the operation, Karinla.” – Epilogue (Crisis). Penfold’s last words as a human, ordering his own death by neurotoxin so Karinla can transplant his brain into Kiwi’s corpse.

    “I have control of the Orb of Life! The pain is gone. I’m truly immortal. Karinla, we did it!” – Epilogue (Crisis). Penfold’s first words after waking in Kiwi’s body, achieving his life’s obsession and setting up the central threat for Book 3.

  • Relationships:

    • Karinla: His assistant, collaborator, and the person he trusts most in the world. She is a spider-woman (arachne) healer and statistical analyst who speaks exclusively in the third person. He vouches for her to Murderjoy: “Karinla’s in this for the science. She believes in what I’m doing and has no interest in escape.” She is uncollared and unreduced – unprecedented for a non-human in Zhakara – because Penfold insisted. In the epilogue, she performs the brain-transfer surgery that grants him immortality, and is genuinely excited rather than repulsed by the procedure.
    • [Queen [Aralynn Murderjoy](./character-queen-aralynn-murderjoy.html)](./character-queen-aralynn-murderjoy.html): His patron and employer. She provides unlimited resources for his immortality research in exchange for weapons development and the Kiwi conditioning project. He speaks to her with a casual rudeness that would be fatal for anyone else, but which she tolerates because his genius is indispensable. Their relationship is transactional rather than loyal – when Tasha raids the lab, Penfold immediately trades state secrets for his freedom.
    • Princess Kiwistafel Questgiver: His primary experimental subject in the Grey Room. He kills and resurrects her repeatedly to map the statistical range of her personality traits, seeking a respawn where her stats align to make her susceptible to Murderjoy’s persuasion. He refers to her as “the subject” and treats her deaths as data points. In the epilogue, her corpse becomes the vessel for his transplanted brain – the ultimate violation.
    • Deirdre: His favourite elven slave servant. She is blonde, quiet, does what she is told without error, and “doesn’t ask questions beyond her meager capability to understand.” She hands him his walking stick and fetches his medicine – a portrait of the compliant domestic servant that Penfold’s Zhakara produces from its systematic destruction of elven personality.
