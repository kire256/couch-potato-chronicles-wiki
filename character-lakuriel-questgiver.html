Lakuriel Questgiver

Source: wiki.md

← Back to Characters View relationship charts

Appears in: Couch Potato Chaos

Profile

  • Appears in: Couch Potato Chaos
  • Aliases: King Lakuriel, the Second King
  • Role: Second king of Questgivria; father of [King [Iolo Questgiver](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)
  • Personality: Lakuriel is a historical shadow — the wiki provides no direct characterisation, no dialogue, and no story scenes featuring him. What can be inferred is modest but meaningful: he inherited the Questgiver throne and the Entropy Bargain from Lorien, ruled as the second king, and produced an heir (Iolo) who would become the most consequential monarch since Lorien himself. A king who rules between the founder and the saviour is, by definition, a steward — maintaining the dynasty and the kingdom during whatever centuries separated Lorien’s bargain from Iolo’s orb quest. His absence from the narrative suggests he was competent enough to avoid catastrophe but not dynamic enough to generate legend. He passed the ticking clock of the Entropy Bargain to his son without solving it, as every Questgiver king before Iolo apparently did.
  • Backstory: Lakuriel was the second king of Questgivria, succeeding Lorien Questgiver — the founder who led the elves to Etheria and struck the Entropy Bargain. His relationship to Lorien is not explicitly stated (son, grandson, or more distant descendant), but the direct succession suggests he was Lorien’s immediate heir. He ruled Questgivria from [Brightwind Keep](./location-brightwind-keep.html) during the Third Age — the era of resurrection and the countdown — and fathered Iolo, who would become the king to launch the orb quest. The wiki notes him only as “the second king of Questgivria and father of [King [Iolo Questgiver](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)](./character-iolo-questgiver.html),” making him the least-documented member of the Questgiver dynasty. His reign is defined entirely by what he inherited and what he passed on: the throne, the Entropy Bargain, and the generational duty to prepare for the countdown that would one day reach zero.
  • Significant story events:
    • The Questgiver Succession (Third Age): Lakuriel’s primary story significance is structural — he bridges the gap between Lorien’s founding bargain and Iolo’s orb quest. His reign maintained the dynasty and ensured that Iolo inherited both the throne and the knowledge of what the Entropy Bargain meant.

  • Relationships:
    • Lorien Questgiver: Predecessor and (presumably) father. Lakuriel inherited the throne and the Entropy Bargain from the first king.
    • [King [Iolo Questgiver](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)](./character-iolo-questgiver.html): Son and successor. Iolo carries the weight of the dynasty’s accumulated dread — the bargain Lorien struck, the countdown that Lakuriel passed on, and the orb quest that Iolo himself must launch.
    • Princess Kiwistafel: Granddaughter. Kiwi’s ability to claim the Orb of Life may ultimately resolve the crisis that Lakuriel — like every intermediate king — could not.
  • Image Generation Prompt: A high-elven king of the early-to-mid Third Age, depicted in the style of a formal royal portrait or dynastic tapestry rather than an action scene — befitting a monarch known only through historical record. He has the characteristic features of the Questgiver line: tall and slender with the dignified build of high-elven royalty, sharply pointed ears, and aristocratic bone structure — high cheekbones, straight nose, strong jaw. Skin is fair with the faint luminous quality of high-elven lineage. Hair is silver-white to pale gold (the family trait), worn in a more formal style than Lorien’s wild journeyman look — perhaps pulled back or bound, reflecting a king who rules from a settled court rather than leading an exodus. Eyes are a cool blue or pale grey, thoughtful and measured — the look of a steward-king rather than a founder or a warrior. His attire is recognisably Questgiver royal: a long robe in deep midnight blue with silver embroidery, similar to Iolo’s but slightly less ornate, suggesting the formalisation of the dynasty’s heraldic traditions over generations. A circlet of silver rests on his brow — the same crown that will pass to Iolo, establishing visual continuity across the dynasty. Expression is calm, dutiful, and faintly weary — the face of a king who knows the clock is ticking but has no answer for it. Background suggestion: the throne room of [Brightwind Keep](./location-brightwind-keep.html) in an earlier era, or a stylised heraldic backdrop with the Questgiver crest. Style: Western fantasy illustration, formal and portrait-like, cool royal colour palette (deep blues, silver, pale gold) with a sense of dynastic continuity.
