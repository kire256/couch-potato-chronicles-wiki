Entropy

Source: wiki.md

← Back to Characters View relationship charts

Appears in: Couch Potato Chaos

Profile

  • Appears in: Couch Potato Chaos

  • Cross-book continuity: also referenced in Crisis

  • Aliases: The Eidolon of Destruction, the god of destruction, the overgrown snake (derogatory, from Dourmal)

  • Role: One of the four eidolons — the most feared. Entropy is the eidolon of destruction, the cosmic force whose ancient bargain with Lorien Questgiver gave Etheria the gift of resurrection in exchange for the world’s eventual annihilation. The countdown that drives the series is the ticking clock on Entropy’s contract.

  • Personality: Entropy is the most terrifying force in the Couch Potato Chronicles universe — a cosmic entity that does not negotiate, does not compromise, and does not relent. The bargain it struck with Lorien was not an act of generosity but of cosmic accounting: resurrection now, destruction later. Entropy is patient in a way that only an immortal force can be — it encircles the sky as a colossal serpent, slowly tightening its coils as the countdown progresses, waiting with absolute certainty for the moment when it will “encircle the world and give a great squeeze” and “squash out all life and crush it with ease.” It does not hate. It does not desire. It simply is — the inevitable end built into the very fabric of the bargain that created Etheria’s resurrection system. [King Dourmal](./character-dourmal.html) dismisses Entropy as “an overgrown snake” who “can’t stand against the mountain kingdom,” but this is the hollow bravado of a paranoid king whistling past the graveyard. Even [Doctor Penfold](./character-doctor-theodore-penfold.html), in his final moments as a dying human, acknowledges Entropy as a personal threat: “Entropy wants my life.” The entity’s presence is felt across both books as the shadow over every victory, every resurrection, every moment of hope — a reminder that the gift of second chances came with an expiry date for the entire world.

  • Backstory: As one of the four fundamental eidolons alongside Catalyst, Libra, and [Snickers the Bumble](./character-snickers-the-bumble.html), Entropy has existed since before Etheria’s recorded history. The eidolons are not created beings but intrinsic cosmic forces — constants given form and will. Entropy’s domain is destruction: the annihilation of matter, life, and meaning. When the elves first arrived in Etheria led by Lorien Questgiver, they were mortal. At some point during or after the migration, Lorien was confronted by Entropy — visible as a colossal serpent encircling the sky — and a bargain was struck, mediated by [Snickers the Bumble](./character-snickers-the-bumble.html). The terms: Entropy would grant the world the gift of resurrection (save points, respawning), but at a future date, it would return to destroy everything. The countdown began. This Entropy Bargain — also called the Lorien Pact — is the foundational event of Etherian civilisation, more consequential than any war, kingdom, or hero. Every save point in the world, every resurrection, every second chance exists because Entropy permitted it in exchange for the world’s eventual destruction. The resurrection system has an implicit age limit: when [Mad Marina](./character-mad-marina.html) dies at 107, the wiki notes that “Entropy will not resurrect her,” suggesting the bargain’s gift has boundaries even Entropy will not extend. By the time of the Couch Potato Chronicles, the countdown is visible on every clock in Etheria, and the orb quest — gathering the elemental orbs to open the Hallowed Chapel — represents the only known path to breaking Entropy’s contract.

  • Significant story events:

    • The Entropy Bargain (Second/Third Age transition): The defining act of cosmic history. Lorien Questgiver negotiates with Entropy — mediated by [Snickers the Bumble](./character-snickers-the-bumble.html) — trading the world’s eventual destruction for the gift of resurrection. This bargain defines every subsequent event in the series: the countdown, the orb quest, the existential dread that permeates Etherian life, and the very game-like mechanics (save points, respawning) that make the world function.
    • Chapter 2 — The Inverted Clock (Chaos): The mysterious countdown appears on clocks across both Earth and Etheria, replacing ordinary timekeeping. The countdown is publicly linked to Entropy’s approach and the deadline on the ancient bargain.
    • Chapter 7 — The Jester and the King (Chaos): Snickers delivers his rhyming prophecy to Brightwind’s court, explaining what Entropy will do: “He will encircle the world and give a great squeeze, he’ll squash out all life and crush it with ease. Naught will remain when he is all through, when the god of destruction has collected his due.” King Iolo reacts with grim recognition: “So the past has caught up with us at last.”
    • Chapter 20 — [Brightwind Keep](./location-brightwind-keep.html) (Chaos): Iolo formally launches the orb quest, explaining that gathering the elemental orbs and opening the Hallowed Chapel may be the only way to end Entropy’s contract. The war council transforms scattered dread into a structured mission.
    • Chapter 35 — They Call Me Player (Chaos): [King Dourmal](./character-dourmal.html) is interviewed and dismisses Entropy’s return as “the troubles of abovegrounders,” claiming “no overgrown snake can stand against the might of the mountain kingdom.” The interview reveals the gap between those who take the threat seriously and those in denial.
    • Chapter 27 — [Mad Marina](./character-mad-marina.html) and the Phantom Child (Chaos): Marina’s death at 107 confirms that the resurrection system has limits: “Entropy will not resurrect her.” The bargain’s gift is not absolute.
    • Crisis — A masked Knight of Entropy: In Couch Potato Crisis, a masked figure claiming the title “Knight of Entropy” seizes the Zhakaran city of Ironfall, reversing its human-over-elf hierarchy. Whether this figure has any actual connection to the eidolon or merely appropriates its name and symbolism is unclear, but the title underscores how Entropy’s presence pervades the political landscape even beyond the countdown.
    • Epilogue (Crisis): [Doctor Penfold](./character-doctor-theodore-penfold.html), dying of organ failure, acknowledges Entropy as a personal adversary: “Entropy wants my life, but we shall deny him his prize.” He orders Karinla to kill him with neurotoxin so she can transplant his brain into Kiwi’s corpse — a final, defiant act against Entropy’s claim on his life.

  • Notable Quotes:

    “He will encircle the world and give a great squeeze, he’ll squash out all life and crush it with ease. Naught will remain when he is all through, when the god of destruction has collected his due.” — Chapter 7, The Jester and the King (Chaos). [Snickers the Bumble](./character-snickers-the-bumble.html) delivers this rhyming prophecy describing Entropy’s endgame. It is the most detailed description of Entropy’s intentions in the series.

    “Rumors don’t interest me. Aye, I’ve heard talk of Entropy from visitors to the mountain, but the troubles of abovegrounders such as yourselves don’t concern us.” — Chapter 35, They Call Me Player (Chaos). [King Dourmal](./character-dourmal.html) dismisses the existential threat, calling Entropy a mere rumour.

    “Entropy wants my life, but we shall deny him his prize. Begin the operation, Karinla.” — Epilogue (Crisis). [Doctor Penfold](./character-doctor-theodore-penfold.html)'s last words as a human, framing his own death as a defiance of the eidolon’s will.

  • Physical description: Entropy is visible as a colossal serpent encircling the sky — a being so vast that it forms a ring around the horizon, visible from anywhere in Etheria. The serpent’s scales are not described in detail in the wiki, but its size is emphasised repeatedly: it is “colossal,” it “encircles the world,” and it is large enough to “squash out all life” with a single constriction. The imagery is that of a world-eating snake — an ouroboros on a planetary scale, slowly tightening its coils as the countdown progresses. [King Dourmal](./character-dourmal.html)'s dismissive reference to it as an “overgrown snake” inadvertently captures its essence: a serpent that has grown beyond any mortal scale, now large enough to encompass the entire world.

  • Image Generation Prompt: A colossal serpent encircling a fantasy world, seen from a mountain vantage point at dusk. The serpent is impossibly vast — its body forms a complete ring around the horizon, with its head visible on one side and its tail disappearing into clouds on the other. Its scales are deep obsidian black with veins of dark purple and deep crimson running through them, like cooling magma or dying stars. The eyes are the size of cities, glowing with a dim, malevolent amber light — not fiery but cold and ancient, the colour of a dying sun. The serpent’s mouth is closed, its jaw muscles tense, as though preparing to constrict. Below it, the fantasy landscape of Etheria stretches in all directions — forests, mountains, castles, and rivers, all tiny beneath the creature’s shadow. The sky between the serpent’s coils is a deep twilight blue, with the first stars appearing. The overall impression is one of cosmic inevitability — this is not a monster that can be fought but a force of nature that can only be delayed. Style: epic fantasy illustration, vast scale, dark palette with the serpent dominating the sky, atmospheric and foreboding.

  • Relationships:

    • Lorien Questgiver (bargaining partner): The defining cosmic transaction. Lorien traded the world’s future for his people’s present survival. Their relationship is transactional and absolute — Entropy honoured its side of the bargain (resurrection), and expects Lorien’s descendants to honour theirs (the world’s destruction).
    • [Snickers the Bumble](./character-snickers-the-bumble.html) (fellow eidolon, mediator): Snickers served as pact mediator during the Entropy Bargain, standing between Lorien and Entropy during negotiations. The timeline describes Snickers as a “partial counterweight to Entropy’s influence,” suggesting the eidolon of mischief actively opposes or mitigates the eidolon of destruction.
    • Catalyst and Libra (fellow eidolons): The four cosmic entities form a system — destruction, change, information, and mischief. Catalyst’s domain of transformation stands in direct opposition to Entropy’s domain of annihilation.
    • [King [Iolo Questgiver](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)](./character-iolo-questgiver.html) (contract inheritor): Iolo carries the weight of the Entropy Bargain inherited from Lorien through Lakuriel. His entire reign is shaped by the countdown, and his defining act — launching the orb quest — is a direct attempt to break the contract.
    • Princess Kiwistafel (potential contract-breaker): As the latest Questgiver heir and the person uniquely able to claim the Orb of Life, Kiwi may hold the key to ending Entropy’s contract. The orb quest that dominates both books is ultimately about finding a way to defy Entropy.
    • The resurrection system (creation): Every save point, every respawn, every second chance in Etheria exists because Entropy permitted it. The entire game-like framework of death and rebirth is Entropy’s gift — and its price.
    • [Doctor Penfold](./character-doctor-theodore-penfold.html) (defier): Penfold explicitly frames his own death as a defiance of Entropy’s claim, suggesting that even a mortal villain can resist the eidolon’s will through ingenuity and ruthlessness.
