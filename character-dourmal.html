Dourmal

Source: wiki.md

← Back to Characters View relationship charts

Appears in: Couch Potato Chaos, Couch Potato Crisis

Profile

  • Appears in: Couch Potato Chaos, Couch Potato Crisis

  • Aliases: Dourmal, King Dourmal, King Under the Laundry Mountain

  • Full name: Dourmal, son of Thronin

  • Cross-book continuity: Referenced in both books. Active offstage presence throughout; never appears “on screen” as a scene character, but his actions drive major plot threads.

  • Role: King Under the Laundry Mountain, orb bearer (Orb of Earth), tyrannical dwarven monarch whose paranoia reshaped dwarven civilisation and exiled the dragons. He is [Prince Hermes](./character-prince-hermes.html)'s father and the primary reason Hermes was raised by the elves of Questgivria.

  • Personality: Dourmal is defined by a corrosive paranoia that metastasised after he inherited the Orb of Earth. He sees enemies everywhere — in his court, in his allies, and ultimately in his own newborn son. His interview with the Sunwell Gazette reveals a king who is curt, dismissive, and quick to correct those who fail to address him by his title (“It’s King Dourmal, missy”). He is xenophobic and isolationist, dismissing “abovegrounders” and their concerns as beneath his notice. Beneath the bluster lies a ruler who has withdrawn from public life entirely — hill dwarves outside the mountain note that the king is rarely seen and that his staff handles day-to-day operations. He is casually bigoted (referring to elves as “pointy-eared bastards” before catching himself mid-slur with a half-hearted “no offense, lass”). His use of the Orb of Earth to “dig deeper and more greedily than any of his predecessors” suggests avarice compounded by the artifact’s corrupting influence. The Orb may have amplified existing personality flaws into full-blown tyranny — Tasha later theorises that the elemental orbs function as “villain generators” that warp their bearers into antagonists.

  • Backstory: Dourmal is the son of Thronin and inherited the throne of the Laundry Mountain — the great dwarven kingdom deep within Dwarselvania. For centuries before his reign, dwarves and dragons coexisted within the mountain in a symbiotic arrangement: dwarves mined precious ore and crafted treasures; dragons protected the hoard and the kingdom from invaders. When Dourmal ascended to the throne, he also inherited the Orb of Earth, an artifact granting command over the element of Earth. The orb’s influence appears to have been decisive. Dourmal decided that dragons provided nothing of immediate monetary value — they slept on gold and consumed vast amounts of food — and concluded the dwarves no longer needed their protection. Rather than negotiate a new arrangement, he used the Orb’s power to slaughter the dragons in their sleep, reinforced the mountain with earth magic, and seized the crystalline dragon eggs as hostages to deter retaliation from survivors. The surviving dragons were displaced into nomadic life, eventually establishing the small settlement of Dragonholm at the North Pole. Dourmal then closed the mountain to all outsiders, cutting the kingdom off from the rest of the world. His paranoia intensified over time: he spent entire weeks in solitude and began seeing threats in every shadow. When his son Hermes was born, Dourmal perceived the infant as a future rival and attempted to kill him before he could be registered at a save point — swinging a great mythril axe at the newborn. The blow missed its mark but severed Hermes’s arm. A dwarven nursemaid rescued the infant, registered him at a save point (locking his one-armed template permanently), and fled with him to the elves of Questgivria, who raised him as their own.

  • Significant story events:

    • The Dragon Slaughter (backstory): Dourmal used the Orb of Earth to murder the dragons of the Laundry Mountain in their sleep and seize their crystalline eggs as hostages. This act destroyed a centuries-old alliance, created an enduring enmity between dwarves and dragons, and forced dragonkind into nomadic exile.
    • Attempted Infanticide (backstory): After the birth of his son Hermes, Dourmal’s paranoia reached its apex. He attacked the newborn with a mythril axe, intending to kill him before save-point registration could make him immortal. The blow severed Hermes’s arm but failed to kill him. The nursemaid’s rescue and the save-point registration locked Hermes’s one-armed form permanently — every subsequent respawn restores everything except the arm.
    • Chapter 20 — [Brightwind Keep](./location-brightwind-keep.html) (Chaos): Dourmal does not appear, but his backstory is revealed through the “Who’s Who in Royalty” newspaper excerpt and Hermes’s own account to Tasha. The newspaper piece documents Dourmal’s paranoia, the dragon exile, and the attempted filicide. Hermes recounts the full history of dwarven-dragon relations and his father’s betrayal, giving Tasha (and the reader) the political context for why dragons and dwarves cannot share an alliance.
    • Chapter 35 — They Call Me Player (Chaos): Dourmal is interviewed by Aranrhod Kefling of the Sunwell Gazette in a rare on-page appearance, conducted deep within the Laundry Mountain. He dismisses concerns about Entropy’s return as “the troubles of abovegrounders,” claims the mountain is safe because “no overgrown snake can stand against the might of the mountain kingdom,” and insults elves as “pointy-eared bastards.” When asked to demonstrate the Orb of Earth’s power to quell rumours that the orb has abandoned him, he abruptly ends the interview with “We’re done here.”
    • Chapter 2 — Tasha’s Bad Day (Crisis): Dourmal is referenced when Penryth the elder red dragon reminds Hermes of his promise to depose his father, become the new King Under the Laundry Mountain, and return the dragons to their ancestral home. Hermes grumbles that he’s “not keen on being king, but somebody has to stop Dourmal.”
    • Epilogue (Crisis): Dourmal is cited by Tasha as a case study in her “villain generator” theory: “King Dourmal used the Orb of Earth to betray his allies and close the Laundry Mountain to outsiders.” He is noted as still holding the Orb of Earth, and Ari observes that “King Dourmal still has the Orb of Earth, and we have no way to retrieve it.” The question of who will remove Dourmal from power and reclaim the orb is left as an open thread for future books.

  • Notable Quotes:

    “Rumors don’t interest me. Aye, I’ve heard talk of Entropy from visitors to the mountain, but the troubles of abovegrounders such as yourselves don’t concern us.” — Chapter 35, They Call Me Player (Chaos). Dourmal dismisses the existential threat of Entropy.

    “Well, I can’t say either way. Of course, I wouldn’t put it past those pointy-eared bastards to destroy the world for their own personal gains. Eh… no offense, lass.” — Chapter 35, They Call Me Player (Chaos). Dourmal’s xenophobic contempt for elves, delivered to an elven interviewer.

    “We’re done here.” — Chapter 35, They Call Me Player (Chaos). Dourmal terminates the interview when asked to demonstrate the Orb of Earth, revealing that the rumours about his bond with the orb failing may be true.

    “Pfft. Ridiculous. I don’t respond to such baseless rumors.” — Chapter 35, They Call Me Player (Chaos). Dourmal denies that the Orb of Earth has turned against him — but refuses to prove it.

    “King Dourmal used the Orb of Earth to betray his allies and close the Laundry Mountain to outsiders.” — Epilogue (Crisis). Tasha cites Dourmal as evidence for her theory that the orbs are “villain generators.”

  • Image Generation Prompt: A dwarven king in his apparent late middle age — grizzled, stocky, and radiating suspicion. Approximately 4’8" (142 cm) with the broad, dense build characteristic of mountain dwarves: barrel-chested, thick-armed, and heavy-set despite not being fat — just compact and formidable. His face is weathered and hard, with deep lines carved by decades of paranoia and isolation. A broad, prominent nose and heavy brow ridge that casts his eyes in perpetual shadow. Small, suspicious dark eyes that dart rather than rest on any single point. His beard is iron-grey to black, thick and wiry, worn long and braided with small metal rings and clasps — dwarven tradition, though the beard shows signs of neglect (stray hairs, uneven trimming) suggesting a ruler who has stopped caring about appearances. Hair is the same iron-grey, worn loose and unkempt beneath a simple dwarven crown of dark iron set with a single earth-toned gem. He wears heavy dwarven armour — dark steel plate etched with geometric runes, worn and functional rather than decorative — over a thick fur-lined cloak. A massive mythril great axe rests across his knees or leans against his throne, its edge gleaming with an otherworldly sharpness. His hands are scarred and thick-fingered, one perpetually gripping the axe handle. A faint earthen glow pulses from the Orb of Earth when it is near — a sphere of deep brown and amber, like compressed soil and crystal, that he keeps chained to his belt or clutched in his palm. His throne room is hewn from living rock deep within the Laundry Mountain: vast stone columns, flickering forge-light, and the oppressive weight of miles of mountain above. Style: Western fantasy illustration, dark and earthy palette — deep browns, iron greys, forge-amber highlights. Menacing, claustrophobic atmosphere.

  • Relationships:

    • [Prince Hermes](./character-prince-hermes.html) (son): Dourmal attempted to kill Hermes at birth, seeing his own infant son as a future threat. The attack severed Hermes’s arm and led to the child being smuggled to the elves. Hermes was raised by [King [Iolo Questgiver](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)](./character-iolo-questgiver.html) in Brightwind and considers Dourmal an enemy, though he grudgingly accepts the obligation to depose him and take the throne. Their relationship is defined by the original act of violence — Hermes bears the permanent physical reminder, and Dourmal’s paranoia made reconciliation impossible.
    • Thronin (father): Dourmal’s predecessor as King Under the Laundry Mountain. Little is known about Thronin, but Dourmal inherited both the throne and the Orb of Earth from him.
    • The Dragons (former allies, now enemies): Dourmal’s slaughter of the sleeping dragons and seizure of their eggs represents the greatest betrayal in dwarven-dragon history. The surviving dragons despise all dwarves because of Dourmal’s actions, and even tolerate Hermes’s presence only barely. The dragon eggs remain hostage within the mountain, a constant point of tension.
    • Penryth (elder red dragon): The leader of the displaced dragons, who holds Hermes to his promise to remove Dourmal from power and return the dragons to the Laundry Mountain.
    • [King [Iolo Questgiver](./character-iolo-questgiver.html)](./character-iolo-questgiver.html): Fellow monarch and the ruler who took in the exiled infant Hermes. The political dynamic between Brightwind and the Laundry Mountain is strained by Dourmal’s isolationism and the fostering arrangement that gave the elves a claim on the dwarven succession.
    • The Orb of Earth: The elemental artifact that Dourmal inherited and wielded to reshape the mountain kingdom. Its influence may have driven his paranoia and tyranny — Tasha theorises that the orbs corrupt their bearers into villains. Rumours among the hill dwarves suggest the Orb has turned against Dourmal and no longer answers to him, a claim he refuses to address.
    • The Hill Dwarves of Dwarselvania: Dwarves who live outside the mountain and have been cut off from the mountain kingdom by Dourmal’s isolationism. They circulate rumours about the king’s failing bond with the Orb and his increasing withdrawal from public life.
